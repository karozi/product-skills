# Pivot Decision Rules

Reference for pivot-or-persevere. Read fully before preparing or running the meeting.

## Persevere conditions (all must hold)

1. **Cohort trend is improving.** Later cohorts activate, retain, or convert better
   than earlier ones. The engine works; it needs tuning, not replacing.
2. **The engine of growth is identified and healthy for its type.** Sticky: churn is
   falling. Viral: viral coefficient is rising toward 1. Paid: LTV:CAC is trending
   above 3. An unidentified or sick engine is not a persevere finding.
3. **Learning milestones are being met.** Each build-measure-learn loop ends in
   validated learning about the riskiest current assumption. If loops end in opinions,
   the process is broken regardless of direction.
4. **There is a specific, plausible next experiment** that could improve the metric
   that matters. "Keep iterating" without a named experiment is not a plan.

## Pivot conditions (any one is sufficient)

1. **Flat cohorts after real tuning.** Multiple tuning iterations have not moved
   activation, retention, or conversion across successive cohorts. The engine is
   working as designed and the design is wrong.
2. **The hypothesis was validated as false.** The riskiest assumption was tested
   cleanly and failed its pre-registered threshold. Acting surprised is not an option;
   the threshold was signed.
3. **Product-market fit signals absent past the expected window.** No word-of-mouth
   growth, no unprompted user passion, no retention curve flattening above the baseline
   — after the runway-adjusted window the team committed to.
4. **The team keeps explaining the metrics.** If every cohort review opens with a
   paragraph of context before the numbers, the numbers have already voted.
5. **Hypothesis drift without process.** The product has pivoted three times informally
   (new segment, new message, new feature focus) without any of it being decided. That
   is a pivot happening in the dark; make it explicit.

## Inadmissible evidence

- Sunk cost: "we've already invested [N months / money]."
- Cumulative metrics presented as progress (run vanity-metric-audit first).
- Board or investor comfort, press interest, competitor moves.
- Exhaustion: "we're too close to stop now."
- Unfalsifiable hope: "the market isn't ready yet" — if the market is the explanation,
  the market must appear in the test plan.

## What a valid pivot looks like

A pivot changes one core hypothesis while preserving validated learning. It names: the
new hypothesis, what was learned that justifies the change, and the first cheap test of
the new hypothesis. The ten classical pivot types (zoom-in, zoom-out, customer segment,
customer need, platform, business architecture, value capture, engine of growth,
channel, technology) are the vocabulary for naming the change — but selecting the type
and designing the next test are a separate step, not part of this verdict.

## The flake test

An extension ("another quarter") is valid only if it names: the metric, the threshold
value, the review date, and the forced action when the tripwire fires. Missing any of
the four, it is a flake and gets reported as one.

sections) and chapter 11 ("Adapt" — the pivot vocabulary); Marc Andreessen's
product-market-fit signals as commonly summarized. Format adapted for this repository.
