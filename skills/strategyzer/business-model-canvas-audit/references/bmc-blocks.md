# The 9 BMC Blocks: Definitions and Guiding Questions

Block names and definitions follow Strategyzer, the canonical source for the Business Model Canvas
(strategyzer.com/library/what-is-a-business-model and strategyzer.com/business-models-the-toolkit-to-design-a-disruptive-company).
Use these names verbatim. Do not rename, merge, or reorder blocks.

For each block: the definition, the guiding questions from Strategyzer, and the evidence types that
typically answer it in a public-evidence audit.

## 1. Customer Segments
Who the company creates value for.
- Questions: For whom are we creating value? Who are our most important customers?
- Public evidence: pricing page tier names (free vs. team vs. enterprise), case studies, job posts naming customer-facing roles, press quotes about target market, app-store category.
- Typical tags: named segments from pricing tiers are often [SOURCED]; segment mix or share of revenue is usually [UNKNOWN].

## 2. Value Propositions
The bundle of products and services that solves customer problems and differentiates from competitors. Quantitative (price, speed) or qualitative (design, experience).
- Questions: What value do we deliver? Which customer problems are we solving? Why choose us over competitors?
- Public evidence: homepage headline and features, product docs, positioning in press coverage, app-store description.
- Watch for: marketing claims are [SOURCED] as claims, not as delivered reality. Tag what the company says it offers.

## 3. Channels
How the company reaches customer segments to communicate and deliver the value proposition.
- Questions: Through which channels do customers want to be reached? How are we reaching them now? Which channels work best?
- Public evidence: where the product is sold (own site, app stores, sales teams, resellers), distribution partnerships in press releases, job posts for sales or growth roles.

## 4. Customer Relationships
The type of relationship established with each segment: personal, self-service, automated, community; aimed at acquisition, retention, or upselling.
- Questions: What relationship does each segment expect? How costly are they? How integrated with the rest of the model?
- Public evidence: support model (docs, community forum, Slack/Discord, named CSMs), onboarding described in docs, community links in the footer.

## 5. Revenue Streams
The cash generated from each customer segment.
- Questions: For what value are customers willing to pay? How do they pay now? How would they prefer to pay? Which streams are biggest?
- Public evidence: pricing page (models and prices are [SOURCED]), SEC filings' revenue breakdown if public, app-store in-app purchase tiers, funding announcements describing the model.
- Hard rule applies hardest here: never state revenue figures, ARPU, or user counts without a fetched source. Relative size of streams (which is biggest) is almost always [INFERRED] or [UNKNOWN].

## 6. Key Resources
The assets required to make the model work: physical, financial, intellectual, or human. Owned, leased, or acquired from partners.
- Questions: Which resources does our value proposition require? Which channels? Customer relationships? Revenue streams?
- Public evidence: job posts (human), patents and licenses (intellectual), infrastructure choices in engineering blogs (physical/technical), funding raised (financial).

## 7. Key Activities
The crucial actions to operate the model.
- Questions: What key activities does our value proposition require? Channels? Revenue streams?
- Public evidence: job posts (what the company hires people to do), product changelogs and release cadence, engineering blog topics, founder interviews about how they spend time.
- Strategyzer examples for calibration: software development for Microsoft, supply chain management for Dell, problem-solving for McKinsey.

## 8. Key Partnerships
The network of suppliers and partners that makes the model work. Four types: strategic alliances between non-competitors, partnerships between competitors, joint ventures, buyer-supplier relationships.
- Questions: Who are our key partners and suppliers? Which resources do we acquire from them? Which activities do they perform?
- Public evidence: integration directory pages, partnership press releases, marketplace listings, co-marketing announcements.

## 9. Cost Structure
All costs incurred to operate the model. Cost-driven vs. value-driven; fixed vs. variable.
- Questions: What are the most important costs? Which key resources and activities are most expensive?
- Public evidence: SEC filings if public, estimated cloud/infra spend in engineering blogs, headcount from job posts or LinkedIn (only if stated by a source), unit economics stated by founders.
- Almost always the weakest block in a public audit. Expect [INFERRED] with rough cost drivers, not numbers.

## Calibration note
A healthy audit usually lands: Value Propositions, Channels, and Revenue Streams pricing mostly [SOURCED]; Key Activities and Partnerships mixed [SOURCED]/[INFERRED]; segment mix, revenue split, and Cost Structure often [INFERRED] or [UNKNOWN].
