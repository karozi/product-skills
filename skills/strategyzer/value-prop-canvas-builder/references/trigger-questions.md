# Trigger Questions from Value Proposition Design

Use these when research runs dry, a cell has fewer than 3 sourced items, or you need
to widen the query set. They are the trigger questions from *Value Proposition Design*
(Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernard, Smith), aligned with Strategyzer's canvas guidance
(https://www.strategyzer.com/value-proposition).

Do not answer these questions yourself. Turn them into search queries against real
user communities, then tag whatever comes back [SOURCED], [INFERRED], or [ORIGINAL].

## Customer jobs

- What functional job is the customer trying to get done? (perform or complete a
  specific task, solve a specific problem)
- What social job is the customer trying to get done? (trying to look good, gain
  power or status)
- What emotional job is the customer trying to get done? (aesthetics, feel good,
  security)
- What basic need is the customer trying to satisfy? (communication, nourishment)
- What supporting jobs does the customer do around the main job? (search, compare,
  decide, buy, dispose; tell others, review, maintain)

## Customer pains

- How does the customer define "too costly"? (time, money, effort)
- What makes the customer feel bad? What are their frustrations, annoyances,
  headaches?
- How do current solutions underperform for the customer? (missing features,
  bad performance, complaints)
- What are the main difficulties and challenges the customer encounters?
  (understanding features, resistance to change, budget approvals)
- What negative social consequences does the customer fear? (loss of face, power,
  trust, status)
- What risks does the customer fear? (financial, social, technical, physical)
- What is keeping the customer awake at night? What are their common fears?
- What common mistakes does the customer make? (usage mistakes, technology
  choices)
- What barriers are keeping the customer from adopting a solution? (upfront
  investment, learning curve, resistance to change)

## Customer gains

- Which savings would make the customer happy? (time, money, effort)
- What quality levels does the customer expect, and what would be more than
  expected?
- What would make the customer's job or life easier? (flatter learning curve,
  better usability, lower cost of ownership)
- What positive social consequences does the customer seek? (looking smart,
  gaining power or status)
- What is the customer looking for most? (good design, guarantees, specific
  levels of performance)
- What does the customer dream about? What big aspirations do they have?
- How does the customer measure success? (features, performance criteria)
- What would increase the likelihood of the customer adopting a solution?
  (lower cost, lower investment, lower risk, better quality, more of something)

## Pain relievers

- Which of the identified customer pains do the products and services eliminate
  or reduce? Which pains do they NOT address?
- How exactly do they eliminate or reduce each pain? (outsource work, remove
  annoyances, erase things customers find risky)
- How do they fit the customer's definition of "better"?

## Gain creators

- Which of the identified gains do the products and services produce?
- How exactly do they produce each gain? (exceed expectations, outperform
  current solutions, fulfill desires, create positive emotions)
- Which gains do they NOT produce?
