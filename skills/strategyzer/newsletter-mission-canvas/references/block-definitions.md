# Block definitions: Mission Model Canvas, newsletter adaptation

## 1. Canonical structure (verified source)

The Mission Model Canvas is Steve Blank's adaptation of the Business Model Canvas, created for the Hacking for Defense class at Stanford and published by Strategyzer. It serves mission-driven organizations where success is impact, not revenue. The block renames, per [Strategyzer: The Mission Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-mission-model-canvas-an-adapted-business-model-canvas-for-mission-driven-organizations):

| # | Business Model Canvas block | Mission Model Canvas block | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer Segments | Beneficiaries | All segments and value-chain layers become beneficiaries |
| 2 | Value Propositions | Value Propositions | Unchanged; one distinct value proposition per beneficiary |
| 3 | Channels | Deployment | Not just distribution: what it takes to go from MVP to widespread use |
| 4 | Customer Relationships | Buy-in/Support | Whose buy-in is needed and how to earn and keep it |
| 5 | Revenue Streams | Mission Achievement | Value created for the sum of all beneficiaries, the greater good; measured in impact, not dollars |
| 6 | Key Resources | Key Resources | Unchanged |
| 7 | Key Activities | Key Activities | Unchanged |
| 8 | Key Partnerships | Key Partnerships | Unchanged |
| 9 | Cost Structure | Mission Cost/Budget | Costs framed as the budget that funds the mission |

Key source principles to respect:

- Mission models are always multi-sided: several beneficiary types, each with its own value proposition.
- Mission achievement exists only when value is delivered to the end beneficiary.
- Each beneficiary deserves its own value proposition analysis (gains, pains, interactions).

## 2. Newsletter creator adaptation

A solo newsletter creator runs a hybrid: mission (reach, influence, serving readers) plus real revenue. So Mission Achievement becomes a metric panel that mixes growth and income. Block names below keep the mission-canvas logic in creator language.

| Mission Model Canvas block | Newsletter block name | Newsletter definition |
|---|---|---|
| Beneficiaries | Beneficiaries | The three sides of a newsletter business: (a) free readers, (b) paid subscribers, (c) sponsors and partners. Free readers consume and amplify; paid subscribers fund via subscriptions or product purchases; sponsors and partners fund via deals, cross-promotion, or revenue share. |
| Value Propositions | Value Propositions | One per beneficiary. Free readers: signal in noise, a take they cannot get elsewhere. Paid subscribers: depth, tooling, community, or access worth paying for. Sponsors/partners: attention and trust of a specific, hard-to-reach audience. |
| Deployment | Channels | How the value physically reaches each beneficiary: the newsletter platform (e.g. Substack), the website plus search (SEO and GEO, being cited by AI answer engines), social distribution, and the community site. Channels must state reach mechanics, not just names. |
| Buy-in/Support | Reader Relationships | How trust is built and kept per beneficiary: cadence and voice for readers, onboarding and offboarding for paid subscribers, deliverability and reporting for sponsors. Buy-in for creators also means algorithmic and platform buy-in: open rates, recommendations, cross-promo goodwill. |
| Mission Achievement | Mission Achievement Metrics | The creator's definition of impact, both growth and income: subscriber growth toward the 2027-reach goal (100K subs by Dec 2027), free-to-paid conversion toward the conversion goal, and monthly revenue toward the 2026-income goal ($10K/mo). Include current values where known; mark unknowns [ASK]. |
| Key Resources | Key Resources | The creator's backlist of posts, the email list itself, the audience's trust, the personal brand and expertise, the stack (Substack, website on Netlify, Gumroad, community site), and time. |
| Key Activities | Key Activities | Writing and shipping on schedule, editing and research, audience growth experiments (referrals, cross-promos, SEO/GEO optimization), paid-tier and product work, sponsor operations, community moderation. |
| Key Partnerships | Key Partners | Substack (platform and recommendation network), cross-promotion partners and other newsletter operators, sponsors, guest writers or collaborators, and tooling providers (Netlify, Gumroad). State each partner's motivation: reach, revenue, or content. |
| Mission Cost/Budget | Costs | Money: platform fees, Substack's cut of paid subs, Gumroad fees, Netlify hosting, tools, editing or design help. Time: hours per week, which is the solo creator's scarcest budget line. |

## 3. Rules of use

1. Never rename or drop blocks. The 9-block shape is fixed.
2. Every beneficiary needs its own value proposition. A shared one is a gap, not a feature.
3. Mission Achievement Metrics must connect to a Northstar label: `2026-income` ($10K/mo) or `2027-reach` (100K subs by Dec 2027). If a metric serves neither, it is a vanity metric; flag it in gap analysis.
4. Costs include time. A solo creator's cost structure that ignores hours is incomplete.
